Course Description/Information:
Graduate seminar on modern biometric recognition and AI. Topics include face, fingerprint, iris, gait, person reidentification, feature representations, similarity learning, verification and identification, evaluation methodologies, robustness, fairness, privacy, and security. Students read foundational and contemporary literature and complete project-based assignments using modern computer vision and machine learning techniques.

Value Proposition:
Beyond the scope of the application of biometrics, this course helps students establish and enhance skills that a necessary for disciplined research-especially relating to Computer Vision, Artificial Intelligence, and Machine Learning.

How to Be Successful in This Course:
Success in this course requires consistent engagement rather than last-minute preparation. Complete the assigned readings before class, participate actively in discussions (online and in-class), and use class time to ask questions, experiment, and collaborate with your peers. Treat the projects as research investigations rather than simply programming assignments. Students should maintain organized code, meaningful Git commits, experiment logs, and clear documentation throughout the process. You are not expected to know everything at the outset-curiosity, persistence, willingness to experiment, and learning from failed approaches are essential parts of doing research. When you encounter difficulties, use Discord to ask questions and help your classmates, and seek instructor guidance when needed.

AI Use Policy:
Generative AI tools are permitted in this course as learning and research aids, including for brainstorming, programming assistance, debugging, literature exploration, editing, and other appropriate uses. However, students remain fully responsible for the accuracy, originality, and integrity of all submitted work. AI-generated content, code, or ideas should be critically evaluated and iteratively refined by the student rather than submitted without review.

When AI tools make a substantive contribution to coursework, students must document and cite their use. Documentation should identify the tool used, the general purpose of its use, and, when relevant, how the student evaluated, modified, or incorporated its output. For project work, this documentation may be included in the project's README, research notebook, or other appropriate project documentation rather than submitted as a separate assignment.

Students may NOT represent AI-generated work as their own without disclosure, fabricate results or citations using AI, or use AI tools in ways that violate course or University academic-integrity policies. Students are ultimately held accountable for everything they submit, including AI-assisted work.

Required Equipment \& Resources:

- Computing Environment: Students are expected to have access to a computer capable of running a modern Python-based machine learning environment. A personal computer with a reasonably recent CPU, sufficient memory, and adequate storage is sufficient for most coursework.
- GPU Computing: A dedicated GPU is recommended but not required. Many of the course projects will involve deep learning and computer vision workloads for which GPU acceleration is highly beneficial. Students without access to a suitable GPU may use the University of Tennessee's ISAAC high-performance computing environment or other instructor-provided/shared computing resources.
- Software: Course projects will primarily use Python and common scientific computing and machine learning libraries, including PyTorch and related computer vision tools. Students will also use Git for version control and will be expected to maintain reproducible software environments and documentation.
- Remote/HPC Access: Students using ISAAC or other remote computing resources should be comfortable working in a Linux environment and accessing systems through a terminal/SSH. Instruction and starter materials will be provided as needed; prior HPC experience is not required.
- Other Equipment: No specialized biometric sensors or hardware are required. Course projects will primarily use publicly available datasets, existing models, and software-based experiments.

- Participation-Students are expected to actively engage in the intellectual life of the course through in-class discussions, research paper discussions, and asynchronous discussion through Discord. Participation includes asking thoughtful questions, contributing ideas, critically engaging with others' perspectives, and helping peers work through technical or conceptual challenges. - Practice/Reproducibility-Students will develop professional practices for conducting and communicating computational research. Throughout the course, students are expected to use version control (Git), maintain meaningful commit histories and changelogs, document experiments and computational environments, and organize code and data so that their work can be understood and reproduced by others. These practices will be evaluated primarily through project repositories, research logs, documentation, and other project deliverables. The emphasis is on producing clear, organized, and reproducible research rather than on the number of commits or amount of documentation.
